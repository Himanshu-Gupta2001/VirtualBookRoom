Known Delays & Processing Considerations
When you request a contact deletion, the process starts with a customizable Suppression Period (default is 2 days, but can be set to 0). During this time, contacts are suppressed from messaging, but their underlying record exists in a pending queue, causing them to still appear as deletion candidates in queries. Additionally, large bulk requests are processed in asynchronous batches, meaning records clear in stages rather than all at once.

There is no set timeline for how long a contact delete process take to occur contact delete is the lowest priority process on the account and thus can take time to finish.

If a deleted contact continues to exist in Sales/Service Cloud (CRM) and passes your sync criteria, MC Connect will automatically recreate the record during the next sync cycle. To prevent contacts without channel addresses from returning, you must update your CRM sync filters (or remove the record in CRM) before running the deletion in Marketing Cloud.

One important consideration is that deleting a contact from Marketing Cloud does not make the corresponding Salesforce Lead or Contact ineligible for synchronization. If the Salesforce record still meets the criteria configured for the Synchronized Data Source, it can be synchronized back into Marketing Cloud after the deletion. We recommend excluding records from synchronization at the Salesforce/source-filter level before deleting the corresponding Marketing Cloud contact.

This is particularly relevant for contacts without channel addresses. Synchronizing Salesforce Lead and Contact objects can create records in All Contacts even when no email, mobile, or other channel address is available. Therefore, if the same Salesforce records continue to qualify for synchronization, they can potentially be recreated and then qualify for your deletion process again.

Contact Delete itself is asynchronous and physical removal from all related datasets can take additional time depending on account activity and deletion volume. However, logically deleted contacts should not normally continue to appear as active contacts simply because physical deletion is still processing.

Based on the current information, my primary recommendation is to determine whether these records are still qualifying for Salesforce synchronization. If they are, the elevated population may represent records being reintroduced into Marketing Cloud rather than a backlog of previously deleted contacts.
